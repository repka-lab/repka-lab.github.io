What the table shows

Over four weeks, BTC has slid from about 86.5k to about 77.9k, roughly −10%. After the early-October high near 87k, rallies stalled in the 84–85k area (Oct 12, Oct 21). The last attempt, on Oct 29, reached only 81.9k.

The more important feature is the floor. For most of October, the 79–80k zone held: Oct 15 low 79.7k, Oct 24 low 79.1k, Oct 26 low 79.3k. On Thursday 29 October that floor broke in one candle, from an 81.2k open to a 77.8k close. Friday then printed a 76.1k low.

The weekend has been quiet: Saturday’s range was under 700 dollars, and Sunday is drifting a little higher, to about 77.9k. The Monday 00:00 UTC reference will probably land somewhere around 77.5–78.5k. That is below the old floor, which now sits overhead as resistance.

The case for UP

A ~6% drop over two days often gets a partial retrace, and the weekend held above Friday’s low with no retest.
Over the long run, slightly more than half of BTC weeks close higher, so the base rate tilts a hair toward UP.
The US midterm elections fall on Tuesday 3 November. Once election uncertainty resolves, risk assets have often seen relief, and equities have historically done well after midterms. Over a single week, though, that effect is noisy. The crypto-policy implications of the result could also cut either way, because the outcome may shape market-structure legislation.
November has been one of BTC’s stronger months historically, though I treat that as a weak, possibly overfit pattern.

The case for DOWN

Academic work on crypto finds time-series momentum at one- to four-week horizons. Every lookback here is negative: one week (≈80.05k to ≈77.9k), two weeks, and four weeks.
A clean break of a month-long floor, followed by a calm weekend just beneath it, more often looks like a pause than a reversal.
The Oct 29 break came the UTC day after the Oct 27–28 FOMC meeting, going by the 2026 schedule I know. That hints the macro news landed badly, though I can’t confirm the cause from this data.
The bigger backdrop from before my cutoff is a market that peaked near 126k in October 2025 and fell hard into early 2026. October’s fading rallies fit that weaker regime.
The October jobs report is due Friday 6 November at about 13:30 UTC, inside the window. It adds variance in both directions rather than a clear tilt.

Weighing it

The question only asks about the sign of the move, not its size, and over five days BTC’s typical swing (±5% or so) swamps any edge. These signals are weak, so this is close to a coin flip.

Trend persistence plus a freshly broken support level overhead gives DOWN a slight edge. Bounce potential and post-election relief offset part of it. An exactly unchanged price also counts as DOWN, but that hardly matters. I’m calling DOWN with only modest conviction.
