Where things stand

Friday’s close is the reference. The Nasdaq rose 1.09% that day, but won back less than a fifth of Thursday’s points loss, and the index logged its fourth straight weekly decline.

On Thursday the Composite fell 5.5% to 24,315, its steepest one-day drop in 19 months. That put it in correction territory, more than 10% below the 6 October record close of 27,599.89.

The trigger was political rather than a collapse in demand:

Democrats won both chambers of Congress.
Texas’s incoming governor pledged to pause approvals for new data centers.
Voters across 10 states weighed 41 local measures on data-center restrictions.
UBS estimates about 30% of planned 2028 data-center additions are at risk.

None of this is law yet. Permitting is a state-level issue, and the new Congress hasn’t been seated.

The rates backdrop is hostile to growth stocks. After the strong jobs report, the 10-year Treasury yield is near 4.87%. December rate-hike odds are close to 80%.

What’s inside the window

CPI, Tuesday. Consensus has headline CPI up 0.3% and core up 0.2%, after two 0.3% core prints. The Cleveland Fed’s nowcast has core at 0.21%.
PPI, Friday, plus about ten Fed speakers during the week.
CoreWeave, Tuesday after the close. It is seen as the main test of how the data-center buildout is being financed.
Nebius, Cisco, Applied Materials and Cerebras, Thursday. Cisco and Applied together are worth about $700 billion, so Friday’s session is effectively the reaction day. Morgan Stanley downgraded Cisco ahead of the report, arguing expectations leave little room for upside.
Wednesday is Veterans Day, but the stock market stays open.
Anthropic is due to start its IPO roadshow, at a valuation reportedly marked down to about $1.2–1.3 trillion from $2 trillion.
Nvidia reports on 17 November, after this week, so it falls outside the window.

Sentiment and valuation

CNN’s Fear & Greed Index stood at 22 (“extreme fear”) as of 6 November.
In the AAII survey for the week ending 4 November, 48.4% of members were bearish, well above the long-run average.
The semiconductor index trades at about 13 times forward earnings, its cheapest since January 2019.
Several large chip names have RSI readings near 20, a deeply oversold level.

Weighing it

Historically, the Nasdaq has closed higher in somewhat more than half of all weeks. Unlike crypto, stock indexes tend to partly reverse their weekly moves rather than extend them. Extreme-fear readings after a 10% correction have more often come before bounces than before further losses.

CPI may also be lopsided. With a hike already about 80% priced, a 0.2% core print could ease pressure more than a 0.3% print would add to it.

Against that, this looks more like 2022 than a routine dip. The Fed is debating hikes, yields are rising, and a new political risk hangs over the AI buildout. In 2022, extreme-fear readings often failed to mark a bottom. The Thursday-night earnings also mean the week can turn on a single session.

I lean UP on base rates, washed-out sentiment and cheap chips, with low conviction.
