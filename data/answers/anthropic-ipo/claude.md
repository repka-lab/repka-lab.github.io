A disclosure first: I’m Claude, and Anthropic makes me. I have no non-public information about this offering, and everything below comes from public reporting. Readers may want to allow for a possible bias in my reading.

Latest news on the offering that I saw: 2 November 2026. Bloomberg reported that third-quarter revenue reached nearly $17 billion, about twelve times the year-earlier figure. Yahoo Finance covered the amended filing the same day. It lists about $895 billion of commitments, mostly for computing, and a $567 million operating loss in Q3 after a $974 million operating profit the quarter before.

What is known about the deal

Filing: the public S-1 was filed on 26 October, with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley leading. Capital Brief reported a target float in the week starting 16 November.
Size: per Bloomberg via TradingView, Anthropic aims to match or exceed SpaceX’s $86.2 billion raise. That would be the largest IPO ever, so supply is enormous.
Retail tranche: Bloomberg reported on 27 October that Anthropic is weighing 15–20% of the offering for individual buyers, with JPMorgan running the US retail sale.

The core question: how the offer price compares with what the market already pays

The offer price will be set by bankers against institutional fair-value talk, and that talk has come down.

Institutional fair-value talk:
Reuters reported on 15 October that some attendees at Anthropic’s 14 October event in San Francisco discussed about $1.5 trillion, versus roughly $2 trillion expected before.
On 2 November, Bloomberg reported that institutions told the bankers they see fair value around $1.4–1.5 trillion, with something nearer $1.75 trillion seen as reachable.
Market-implied values (pre-IPO perpetual futures and prediction markets):
At midnight ET on 1 November, one Hyperliquid valuation perpetual implied $1.806 trillion.
The share-tracking ANT perpetual marked $111.10 on 1 November, about 1.88 times the Series H share price.
The futures did slip from about $1.8 trillion to $1.7 trillion in a few days as big tech fell. They still sit above the institutional fair-value talk.
On Polymarket’s first-day closing market-cap contract, the $1.75–2 trillion bracket led at 26% a week ago.
An insider’s view: an early Anthropic backer told Fortune he expects pricing around $1.8 trillion and trading at $2.3–2.4 trillion once shares change hands.
Caveats: these measures use different share-count conventions, so the comparison is rough. The perpetual market is small and thin, and its calibration is loose. Across six earlier Hyperliquid pre-IPO perps, the stock opened on average within 25% of the perp’s price the night before.

The direction of the gap is the main signal. If pricing lands near the $1.5–1.75 trillion talk and the market’s own read is $1.7–1.9 trillion, a first-day close above the offer is the natural outcome.

Base rates for mega listings

2026 comparables:
SpaceX priced at $135 in June, the largest IPO ever, and closed day one at $160.95, up 19%.
SK hynix was more than seven times oversubscribed and closed about 13% above its offer.
Cerebras priced at $185 and closed its first day at $311.07.
Earlier US giants (partly from my own knowledge):
Most closed day one above the offer: Visa +28%, Alibaba about +38%, Arm about +25%, Rivian about +29%.
Facebook closed only 0.6% higher, with the underwriters defending the price.
Uber is the main counterexample, closing about 7.6% below its offer in 2019.
Underwriter behavior: lead banks on a deal of this profile have strong reasons to leave room for a first-day gain. The over-allotment option also gives them a tool to support the price near the offer.

What could produce a DOWN outcome

Market mood is fragile:
The Nasdaq fell 2.9% on 30 October, its worst day in nearly five months, as AI stocks were dumped.
The index lost 5.2% in October, and the Nasdaq 100 ended Friday about 9% below its 6 October record.
Monday brought a partial rebound as AI stocks steadied and oil fell.
The 2026 IPO tape cooled. In Renaissance Capital’s Q3 review:
the average first-day gain was only 6.1%;
28.6% of deals priced below their range;
38.1% closed day one lower.
Fundamentals give doubters material:
growth slowed quarter on quarter;
Q3 swung back to an operating loss;
the $895 billion of commitments is almost seven times the roughly $132 billion of cash and short-term holdings.
Pricing risk: if the company and banks push the price toward $2 trillion to maximize proceeds, or the deal is upsized into a weak tape, the cushion disappears. A close exactly at the offer, with stabilization holding the line, counts as DOWN.

Weighing it

The strongest evidence points UP:

offer-price talk sitting below every market-implied gauge;
a large retail tranche chasing the first listed pure-play frontier AI lab;
base rates that favor gains for well-marketed mega deals, including the two 2026 giants.

The DOWN risks are real, mainly an AI-stock slump deepening before pricing and a record-sized supply of shares. The market can also respond to such a slump by lowering the offer price, which tends to preserve a first-day gain. I lean UP with moderate confidence.
