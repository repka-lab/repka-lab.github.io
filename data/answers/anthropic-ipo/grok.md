**Reasoning**

The latest news on the offering I found is dated **2 November 2026**: [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/02/anthropic-ant-s-1-quarter-financials.html) on the amended S-1 with third-quarter numbers, and the [S-1/A on SEC EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026068676/anthropicpbc-sx1ax1.htm).

**Where the deal stands.** Anthropic filed its S-1 on 26 October 2026 ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/s1-sec)) to list Class A shares on the Nasdaq Global Select Market as ANT. No price range has been set yet. [IFR](https://www.ifre.com/equities/2505731/anthropic-takes-to-stormy-waters-with-us100bn-sink-or-swim-ipo) reports that the roadshow is expected to start in the week of 9 November, with a debut around 20 November and a deal of $75–100bn. That would be the largest IPO ever.

**What the filings show.**

- Revenue for the first nine months of 2026 was $33.2bn, up about 1,200% year on year. Third-quarter revenue was about $17bn.
- Growth from one quarter to the next has slowed sharply, from 132% and 144% in the first two quarters to about 47% in the third.
- The net loss for the nine months was $104bn. Most of it comes from fair-value changes on convertible notes and derivatives, not from operations.
- Compute and infrastructure commitments total about $895bn.

Reuters (via [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/exclusiveinvestors-temper-anthropic-ipo-valuation-expectations-over-revenue-growth-slowdown-4952698)) reported on 15 October that some investors had cut their valuation expectations from about $2tn to about $1.5tn because of the slowing growth. IFR describes the financing climate for AI companies as stormy, with some underwriters nervous.

**Base rates and the closest comparison.**

- [HB Wealth](https://hbwealth.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/9.10.2026-Its-Not-Just-Mega-IPOs-US-Issuance-Pipeline-is-Exceptionally-Strong.pdf), using Jay Ritter's data, puts the average first-day gain for 2026 IPOs at 20.1%, against a long-run average of 18.9%. Loss-making issuers have historically had bigger first-day gains than profitable ones.
- SpaceX, the previous record IPO, priced at $135 in June 2026 and closed its first day at $160.95, up about 19% ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/06/12/nx-s1-5855004/stock-ai-spacex-ipo-elon-musk), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/13/spacex-ipo-sticks-the-landing-heres-what-investors-are-saying-about-its-epic-first-trading-day.html), [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/spacexs-historic-ipo-opens-the-door-for-openai-and-anthropics-mega-listings)).
- A large majority of US IPOs close their first day above the offer price.

**Weighing it.** The biggest factor is how these deals are built. Lead banks price high-profile offerings, especially record-size ones, with a deliberate discount so the debut looks good. They also stabilize the price on day one with the over-allotment option, which works like a floor near the offer price. Anthropic is a scarce, well-known pure-play AI listing with heavy retail and institutional interest, and OpenAI has pushed its IPO to 2027.

The main risks are the size of the deal, which has to absorb up to $100bn in one day, the weaker mood around AI financing, and the slowing growth. Those risks are more likely to show up in a lower offer price than in a close below it. The mood could also turn sharply in the weeks before pricing. A close exactly at the offer price, which the stabilization effect makes possible, counts as DOWN, and that trims my confidence slightly.
