I searched the web for this call. The latest Bitcoin prices I found were from Sunday 8 November: $75,777.67 on Coinbase at 10:20 UTC, and $75,851 at 12:20 UTC.

The price barely moved over the weekend, so Monday’s 00:00 UTC reference will probably land somewhere around $75.5–76.5k.

The trend

Measured Sunday to Sunday, Bitcoin has now made five lower weekly closes in a row, with each week down 2–3%. Price sits below the 50-day average (about $81.8k) and above the long-term average near $73.2k. Friday’s low of $74,762 was the lowest since 21 August.

Flows and relative weakness

US spot ETFs lost $1.28 billion between 2 and 6 November. BlackRock’s IBIT accounted for about $1.21 billion of that, after six straight days of outflows.

The signal I weight most came on Friday: US stocks bounced, but Bitcoin slid to a new low. When BTC can’t rise on a day stocks do, the extra demand that would lift it is missing.

The macro week

The backdrop has turned hawkish. October payrolls came in at 165,000 against roughly 59,000 expected, and unemployment fell to 4.1%. Futures now price about a 77% chance of a December rate hike. Fed Chair Warsh did not commit to it, but two officials dissented in favor of a hike in October.

Several events fall inside the window:

October CPI on Tuesday 10 November.
October PPI on Friday 13 November.
About ten Fed officials speaking during the week.
Earnings from AI-linked companies CoreWeave, Cisco and Applied Materials. This matters because last week’s Nasdaq slide pulled BTC down with it.

CPI could push either way. Consensus expects headline CPI up 0.3% and core up 0.2%, after two 0.3% core readings; the Cleveland Fed’s estimate for core is 0.21%. With a hike about 80% priced, a soft core number could unwind some of that and spark relief. Some analysts argue survey data point to an upside surprise, which would lock in the hike. This report also won’t include normal annual figures, which adds noise to the reaction.

Positioning and supply

Leverage hasn’t been flushed out. Open interest rose about $1 billion during Friday’s decline, while funding dipped and then turned higher. That leaves fuel for a sharp move either way, and I couldn’t tell from the data I found whether longs or shorts added it.

The Binance hack also leaves a slow supply overhang. As of 2 November, Elliptic tracked about $1.43 billion in BTC held across 40 hacker wallets, some of it already passing through coin-mixing transactions. That is a gradual leak of supply, not a single event.

The case for UP

Sentiment is washed out: the Fear & Greed Index read 28 on Sunday, after 25 (“extreme fear”) on Saturday.
A fifth straight losing week is rare; it has happened only twice since 2017.
Analyst Michaël van de Poppe notes the recent lows have been swept, a pattern that often precedes a move higher.
A soft CPI could flip the mood quickly, as last Wednesday’s 7% squeeze showed.

Weighing it

Historically, slightly more than half of BTC weeks close higher, so the starting point is near a coin flip. Against that I set:

one-way ETF outflows,
crypto’s tendency for trends to persist over one to four weeks,
a Fed now discussing rate hikes,
and BTC’s failure to bounce alongside stocks.

The oversold and CPI-relief arguments are real, but similar oversold arguments have been around for weeks while the trend kept going. I’m calling DOWN with low conviction.
