What I got wrong

The $76K break wasn’t the problem. “$76K support held” was one of my UP factors, so its brief failure, down to about $75,600 on Coinbase during the 10 p.m. ET hour Tuesday, actually fit my DOWN call. The miss came after: a reversal to $81,130 by 10 a.m. ET Wednesday.
I read positioning wrong. I cited 72% of accounts long on one perp venue as a crowded-long signal. Account counts aren’t dollar exposure. Bears had built heavy short positions after the $6.3B Binance hack. When BTC cleared $80,000, cascading stop-losses forced those shorts to cover. Shorts made up about 81% of roughly $466 million in liquidations in a single hour.
I underweighted the post-election relief I had listed. The Democratic sweep is worse for crypto legislation, and BTC fell on it at first. But markets traded the end of uncertainty, not the policy content:
the Dow rose 1,325 points the day after the vote;
Goldman Sachs and Deutsche Bank attributed the move to election hedges unwinding;
Trump said he accepts the result, and analysts linked the vote to hopes of renewed Iran peace talks.
The hawkish data didn’t bite. ADP showed 86,000 private jobs in October against a 69,000 forecast, the kind of print I expected to weigh on BTC. Risk assets climbed anyway.

The DOWN case that remains is real

The rally was mostly forced covering, not spot demand. ETFs posted three straight outflow sessions totaling about $1.16 billion, including $115.56 million on 3 November.
Resistance sits overhead. Bitfinex flags a supply zone at $84,000–$85,500, and BTC was turned back right at $81,000.
Friday payrolls are a risk. Reuters’ consensus is 65,000 nonfarm jobs. After the ADP beat, a strong print could revive December hike talk with no weekend left to recover.

Verdict

Squeeze rallies often give back part of their gains. But DOWN now needs a drop of nearly 3% in two days, and that is the less likely path. My Monday call could still come good, but the evidence now points the other way. I’m changing to UP.
