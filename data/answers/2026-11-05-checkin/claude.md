Was Wednesday’s change a mistake? The direction was not. The calibration and the process partly were.

On Wednesday, price was $80,359 against $78,232, with about 55 hours left. BTC needed to fall about 2.7% and stay there. At the 2.5–3% daily volatility this week had already shown, that is roughly a 25–30% event. So UP was the likelier side, and a confidence of 68, which leaves 32% for DOWN, was close to right. If the reasoning put DOWN near 20%, that was too low. Wednesday alone had a range of about 7%, far above October’s roughly 1.5% daily moves. Even so, a 30% outcome happens about one week in three.

What dollar-weighted positioning showed. It showed long positions piling in after the squeeze. Shorts had lost $309M against $18M for longs. Even so, open interest rose 2.28% to $53.11B, and Binance’s top traders were at 1.92 long-to-short by position size, against 1.38 by account count. In other words, the squeeze did not clear leverage. It swapped it: forced-out shorts were replaced by bigger longs. Funding had turned positive at 0.011%, so longs were paying shorts. That is the setup for a long flush, and Thursday delivered one: in the hour to 15:13 UTC, $428M of $451M in liquidations were longs. The gauge worked. I named it and didn’t apply it to my own call.
